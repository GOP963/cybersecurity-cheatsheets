

#### Definition:
A Red Team refers to a group of security professionals or ethical hackers who are tasked
with simulating real-world attacks on an organization's systems or infrastructure.

They act like adversaries to test the effectiveness of the organization's defenses.

Focus: Performing adversarial simulation.

#### Example:
A group of skilled penetration testers and offensive security experts hired to simulate a
cyber attack on a company's network.

Red Team refers to the group of professionals carrying out the tests.

***Red Teaming*** refers to the act, process, or strategy of simulating attacks in an
adversarial way.


#### Attack life cycle

The attack lifecycle refers to the various stages an attacker goes through when
conducting a cyberattack.

It helps both attackers understand how to conduct a successful attack and defenders know
how to recognize and stop it at different stages.

#### Tactics, techniques and procedures (TTPs)

Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) refer to the methods and strategies used by
adversaries to carry out cyberattacks.

Tactics, Techniques and Procedures (TTPs) are a key concept in both offensive and
defensive cybersecurity operations.

They describe how adversaries approach and execute attacks, allowing defenders to
understand and counter threats effectively.


#### Tactics

###### Definition:
The tactics refer to the high-level objectives or the overall goal of an attack. They are
the "why" behind an adversary's actions and provide context for their attack.

###### Example:
The goal could be data exfiltration, lateral movement, persistence, or privilege
escalation.

###### Real-World Example:
An attacker may use tactics to gain initial access to a network, such as through phishing
or exploiting vulnerable services.

#### Tactics, techniques and procedures (TTPs)

Understanding TTPs is crucial for both offensive (Red Teams) and defensive (Blue Teams)
cybersecurity operations

###### For Offensive Teams (Red Teams): 😈
Offensive security professionals use TTPs to emulate real-world threats. By mimicking the
tactics, techniques and procedures of sophisticated adversaries, they can identify
weaknesses in an organization's defenses.

###### For Defensive Teams (Blue Teams): 🥱
Understanding the TTPs used by attackers allows defensive teams to build strategies to
detect, prevent, or respond to specific techniques. For example, a defender who knows
that adversaries use phishing to gain initial access can implement better email filtering
and user training to mitigate the risk.
